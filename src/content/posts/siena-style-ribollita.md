# Siena-Style Ribollita

There is nothing quite like a hearty bowl of soup on a cold winter day. This rustic Tuscan soup brings together tender beans, seasonal vegetables, and Parmesan, slowly simmered until rich and velvety. Pieces of rustic bread melt into the broth, creating a wonderfully thick, comforting texture that warms you from the inside out.

In Siena, the soup is traditionally finished with a golden crust of caramelized onions and bubbling Parmesan, much like a French onion soup. It is my favourite way to serve it, especially on a chilly winter evening. Generous and wonderfully satisfying, this recipe is perfect for sharing around the table with family and friends, whether gathered for a cozy evening in, a winter supper, or a relaxed weekend get-together.

## Recipe Details

- **Serves:** 4 to 6
- **Cuisine:** Italian-inspired
- **Category:** Soups & Main Course

## Ingredients

### For the Main

- 4 tablespoons extra-virgin olive oil, plus more for serving
- 1 cup chopped yellow onions
- ½ cup chopped fennel or celery
- ½ cup chopped carrots
- 2 garlic cloves, chopped
- Kosher salt and freshly ground black pepper
- 1 (15-ounce) can cannellini or butter beans, undrained
- 1 large bunch lacinato (Tuscan) kale, tough ends trimmed, chopped into bite-size pieces
- 2 cups low-sodium beef broth
- ½ cup passata (tomato puree)
- 2-ounce chunk Parmigiano-Reggiano cheese, broken into pieces
- 2 ciabatta rolls, preferably a day or two old, cubed (about 2 cups)
- 1 tablespoon red wine vinegar
- 1 small red onion, very thinly sliced
- ⅓ cup freshly grated Parmigiano-Reggiano cheese
- Fennel fronds, for garnish
- Additional olive oil for serving

## Rice 
-2 cups of brown rice 
-4 cups of water 

## Protein & Marinade 

-1/2 lb pork 
- 1/3 cup soy sauce
2 tbsp brown sugar
3 cloves garlic, minced
1 thumb fresh ginger, grated
1–2 tsp sesame oil

Instructions

1. Prepare the Pork by at least 30 mins in advance by thawing a 1/2 lb of pork in a bowl and mixing the marinade ingredients in a separate bowl. One thawed combine and mix until all surfaces are covered. Cover and let rest for 30mins in the fridge.

2. Prepare the Vegetables

In a Dutch oven, heat 2 tablespoons of the oil over medium-low heat. Add the yellow onions, fennel, carrots, garlic, and salt and pepper to taste. Sauté until softened, about 6 minutes.

3. Cook the Beans and Kale

Add half the beans to the pot and use a potato masher to crush them into a rough puree.

Add the remaining beans and their liquid, the kale, broth, passata, Parmigiano pieces, and 2 cups water.

Bring to a boil. Reduce the heat to low, cover, and simmer for 15 minutes.

4. Preheat the Oven

Preheat the oven to 450°F.

5. Fry Pork
In a medium sized frying pan, add oil on high. Once sizzling, add the pork. Cook each side for 2 mins and then reduce heat to medium. Cover and cook completely for about 15 mins, set aside. 
  
6.  Add the Bread to the main

Stir the bread cubes into the Dutch oven, making sure they are submerged in liquid, then cover and simmer until the kale is very soft and the bread is falling apart, another 15 minutes.

7. Cook the Rice
   In a pressure cooker add the water and rice to the pot. Cook as directed, or about 15 mins. 

8. Create Texture

Stir the soup briskly with a whisk to break up the bread cubes and cheese and to create a thick, porridge-like texture.

Stir in the vinegar and more salt and pepper to taste.

9. Prepare the Topping

Arrange the red onion slices over the soup in a single layer and sprinkle evenly with the grated Parmigiano.

Drizzle with the remaining 2 tablespoons olive oil.

10. Bake Until Golden

Transfer to the oven and bake, uncovered, until the onion and cheese are golden brown, about 20 minutes.

11. Serve

Serve rise and pork topped with the main Ribollita and another drizzle of oil, making sure that each serving gets some of the crusty topping.
